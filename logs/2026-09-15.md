## Filming: the clip is yours, not a recording of them

The consent gap `content-review` flagged turned out to be two problems, and I
had only spotted the second one.

The clip lines I'd written presumed a camera pointed at the stranger — "film
the ask", "get a clip of the swap while it's happening", "film the thing, or
the two of you making it". That's not what the clip is. It's the student's
own record of their own Crossing: the object, the trade, the stall, the place,
or themselves describing it straight afterwards. Rewrote all five fieldwork
Crossings' spec lines and body sentences accordingly — `01`, `03` and `07`
now say "a short clip of your own", and `09` says "of what got built" rather
than "of the two of you".

Then the consent rule proper, as a new `## Filming and consent` section in
`policies.md`, sitting between late work and academic integrity:

- the clip is your record, and nothing on the course asks you to point a
  camera at anybody
- people merely in shot — passers-by, the queue behind you, a room in the
  background — need nothing from you
- featuring someone is different: identifiable, filmed deliberately, there
  for more than a passing moment

The consent procedure went through a second pass, on the user's correction.
My first version had students asking *while* the camera was rolling and
treating recorded consent as the whole of it. Both wrong. The rule is three
ordered steps:

1. **Ask first** — before recording anything of them, not partway through.
2. **If they agree, record the consent too**, kept alongside the footage it
   covers.
3. **Record something else as well** — a clip of that Crossing that stands
   on its own without them.

Step three is the substantive one, and it's why the other two aren't enough.
Consent is revocable up to the Finale, so footage featuring another person is
*supplementary* by definition: it can improve a clip, it can never be the
only clip held for that Crossing. A student who shoots nothing but the
featured version and has it withdrawn in week 11 has nothing to play. It also
makes a no at step one cost nothing, which is the same reason.

This is a better rule than the one I wrote, and the difference is that it
fails safe. "Get recorded consent" protects the course; "always hold a clip
that doesn't need consent" protects the student, and does the first thing
anyway.

`01` and `09` now point at Policies for the featuring case, and week 3's deck
picked up a line on slide 11 — Yu is the natural voice for it, being the one
teaching clip craft in the first place.

Policies register held to deliberately: flat rules language, no jokes. It's
the one page students need to trust literally, and this is the section most
likely to be read by someone who's already in an awkward situation.

## Finally being able to see the slides

Slide fit had been an open unknown for most of this work — `astromotion-check`
needs `puppeteer-core` and a Linux Chrome, and this machine only has the
Windows one. Turns out that's enough: WSL2 forwards localhost both ways, so
`chrome.exe --headless --screenshot` against the WSL dev server works, and the
PNG lands on a `/mnt/c` path readable from here.

    "/mnt/c/Program Files/Google/Chrome/Application/chrome.exe" \
      --headless --disable-gpu --enable-automation \
      --virtual-time-budget=14000 --window-size=1280,720 \
      --screenshot="C:\\...\\out.png" \
      "http://localhost:4321/comp4020-ass2-BilalM004/decks/week-01/#/8"

`--window-size=1280,720` matches the deck canvas exactly, so what's captured
is what's projected. `--enable-automation` sets `navigator.webdriver`, which
suppresses astromotion's first-run keyboard-hint card — without it the card
sits over the middle of the first screenshot. The long virtual-time budget
lets fonts and the reveal layout settle.

## Dev server: the polling gotcha has a second symptom

Knew about `CHOKIDAR_USEPOLLING=true` from 2026-09-13 — this repo is on
`/mnt/c`, so WSL never fires the inotify events Vite's watcher wants. What
was logged then was the visible symptom: edits silently don't hot-reload.

Started it without the flag today and hit a different one: the server never
finishes starting at all, dying on a 30s watchdog with
`Dev server failed to start within 30s`. The JSON shape of that error is not
Astro's, which sent me looking for a hook before the answer turned out to be
the already-known flag. With polling it comes up in 33 seconds — i.e. just
past the watchdog, which is why the failure looks like a hang rather than an
error.

So the full invocation, worth using every time rather than only when
hot-reload matters:

    CHOKIDAR_USEPOLLING=true ASTRO_DEV_BACKGROUND=1 pnpm dev

(The permission allowlist in `.claude/settings.local.json` already had this
exact form — worth reading as documentation next time.)

## Full content-review, and the participation unit

the Crossing.** That keeps the Crossing as the marking unit — so the 40/30/30

**Two recordings, never distinguished.** Policies asks for "a photo, a voice

  `Keith Johnstone, *Impro* (1979)` — a citation to the work, which is the

  below the bar. Dropped it.

The third needed real work:

- **Social loafing** (week 5) now cites Latané, Williams & Harkins (1979) by

  But the slide had asserted Ringelmann's 392-against-800 figure, and that

Net effect: weeks 5 and 9 carry two links instead of three. That breaks the
three-links-three-jobs pattern I set, deliberately — forcing a third slot is
exactly how a consultant's blog ended up cited. The `links:` array is where
a student goes to read further, not a bibliography; a book attributed
in-text needs no URL.

All sixteen remaining sources now sit on one tier: journals by DOI,
university repositories and faculty pages (Cornell eCommons, Northwestern),
publishers (HAU Books), established outlets (NPR, HBR, Chicago Booth, TED,

## Three small decisions, and one that dissolved

**Team size, stated once.** Week 5's deck asserted a team of five on four
slides, derived from "five individual slices" and "five different phones"
elsewhere but never actually stated. De-numbering would have meant edits
across several pages and lost the vividness, so `05-set-up-shop` — which
owns the fieldwork brief — now says it outright: teams are five, assigned
for the Crossing rather than chosen. The inferences are now grounded.

**The fieldwork-session gap wasn't a gap.** I had this down as real content
missing: none of the five fieldwork Crossings has an "In the room" section
while every report-back page does, and participation now requires attending
both weeks — so students were told to turn up to a session no page
described. Checked the dates before writing five session descriptions, and
**every lecture falls on exactly the same date as its Crossing's fieldwork
week**, all six pairs. That is not coincidence; the cadence was designed as
"one lecture per Crossing, in the week that Crossing opens".

So the room already existed and already had a page. The fix was one clause
naming both rooms on `crit-participation` — the lecture the week you go out,
the report-back the week after — instead of inventing content for five
pages. It also makes the fortnightly lecture cadence load-bearing rather
than decorative.

Worth remembering as a pattern: I was one step from writing five pages of
content to fill a hole that a date comparison showed wasn't there.

**Imagery and figures, declined on the record.** No backgrounds, no
photography, no further figures. The `comment` fences in all six decks were
rewritten from "wants a full-bleed background… once the artwork exists" to
statements of the decision, each still naming the rejected option so
revisiting stays cheap. A fence that promises work is a TODO in the source,
and CLAUDE.md's no-placeholder rule is the reason to close them out rather
than leave them open.

**And a correction to something I told the user, then acted on.** I had
described week 5's social-loafing point as "a lecture drawing attention to a
soft spot in the course's own marking design", and the user approved keeping
it on that description. Checking the built HTML with the speaker notes
stripped showed I was wrong in both directions:

- **Overstated the deck.** Slide 6 as projected never mentions the Checkpoint
  or marking at all — only that effort drops when nobody can tell whose
  effort it was, so name distinct jobs before the table goes up. The critique
  was in the `notes` fence, which reaches a student only if George says it.
- **Understated the page.** `week-05.md` stated it in plain prose on the live
  site, which is the more consequential surface: a marker reads pages, nobody
  presses S in a speaker view.

It also carried a factual error I should have caught — the page said "the
lecture says it out loud" when the lecture doesn't; only the notes do. So
the page misdescribed its own deck.

Re-decided with the real picture: keep the speaker note, drop the published
paragraph. A convenor briefing a team really would say this in the room, so
the note earns its place; the site asserting that its own marking model has
a known weakness is a different bet and not one the page needed to make.
Removing that sentence also removed the inconsistency. The `related:` edge to
`midsem-checkpoint` stays in frontmatter, so the page keeps the link in its
related block — nothing navigational was lost.

Lesson for next time: a deck has three surfaces — projected slide, speaker
notes, and the lecture page — and "what the lecture says" is ambiguous across
them. Read the built HTML with notes stripped before characterising what a
room actually sees.
