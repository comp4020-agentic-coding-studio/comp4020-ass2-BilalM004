## Filming: the clip is yours, not a recording of them

The consent gap `content-review` flagged turned out to be two problems, and I
had only spotted the second one.

The clip lines I'd written presumed a camera pointed at the stranger — "film
the ask", "get a clip of the swap while it's happening", "film the thing, or
the two of you making it". That's not what the clip is. It's the student's
own record of their own Crossing: the object, the trade, the stall, the place,
or themselves describing it straight afterwards. Rewrote all five fieldwork
Crossings' spec lines and body sentences accordingly — `01`, `03` and `07`
now say "a short clip of your own", and `09` says "of what got built" rather
than "of the two of you".

Then the consent rule proper, as a new `## Filming and consent` section in
`policies.md`, sitting between late work and academic integrity:

- the clip is your record, and nothing on the course asks you to point a
  camera at anybody
- people merely in shot — passers-by, the queue behind you, a room in the
  background — need nothing from you
- featuring someone is different: identifiable, filmed deliberately, there
  for more than a passing moment

The consent procedure went through a second pass, on the user's correction.
My first version had students asking *while* the camera was rolling and
treating recorded consent as the whole of it. Both wrong. The rule is three
ordered steps:

1. **Ask first** — before recording anything of them, not partway through.
2. **If they agree, record the consent too**, kept alongside the footage it
   covers.
3. **Record something else as well** — a clip of that Crossing that stands
   on its own without them.

Step three is the substantive one, and it's why the other two aren't enough.
Consent is revocable up to the Finale, so footage featuring another person is
*supplementary* by definition: it can improve a clip, it can never be the
only clip held for that Crossing. A student who shoots nothing but the
featured version and has it withdrawn in week 11 has nothing to play. It also
makes a no at step one cost nothing, which is the same reason.

This is a better rule than the one I wrote, and the difference is that it
fails safe. "Get recorded consent" protects the course; "always hold a clip
that doesn't need consent" protects the student, and does the first thing
anyway.

`01` and `09` now point at Policies for the featuring case, and week 3's deck
picked up a line on slide 11 — Yu is the natural voice for it, being the one
teaching clip craft in the first place.

Policies register held to deliberately: flat rules language, no jokes. It's
the one page students need to trust literally, and this is the section most
likely to be read by someone who's already in an awkward situation.

## Figures, not photographs

User ruled out full-bleed backgrounds and opened the door to sourcing images
from the internet. Went looking, and came back recommending against it.

Checked what was actually available under a clear licence. The strongest
candidate by subject matter was a potlatch photograph for week 3's Mauss
slide — Commons has a public-domain one from Alert Bay, c.1910. Declined it:
the catalogue record lists it as a tourist postcard, and it was taken during
the period when the potlatch was banned in Canada. Using that to illustrate
undergraduates swapping badges turns a serious thing into decoration. The
remaining licence-clean options (a portrait of Mauss; a CC BY-SA photo of a
book he inscribed for Georges Dumas) are decorative rather than
argumentative, and the second's ShareAlike sits awkwardly against the site's
CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0. On top of all that there's a plain style problem: the whole
identity is illustrated gold/black/cream halftone, and a photograph reads as
pasted in.

Put the case to the user, who agreed. Built three inline-SVG figures instead,
coloured from the `--at-*` tokens:

- `FigCrossing` (week 1, slide 7) — twelve weeks as six Crossings, an
  outlined "out" week paired with a filled "back" week, the sixth pair broken
  because Crossing 6 has no fieldwork. Structure, count and exception in one
  graphic.
- `FigForecast` (week 1, slide 13) — the Epley reversal as a single line from
  predicted-worst to actually-best.
- `FigSpine` (week 3, slide 15) — the four-beat story spine with "one day"
  filled, since that's the beat the slide says a dull trade is missing.

`FigForecast` is the one worth being careful about, and it plots no
magnitudes and ranks no condition other than the one the source actually
reports. A proper three-way bar chart would look more authoritative and
would be inventing data — the release gives no effect sizes. The figure
captions itself to that effect, so the restraint is visible rather than
looking like an omission.

Each figure earned its slide's prose down: the reversal, the five-and-one
structure and the spine were all being spelled out in sentences the graphic
now carries. That also buys vertical space back on three of the slides
flagged as tight, which is a second reason to prefer a figure over a photo —
a photo would have cost space rather than saved it.

Verified in `dist/`: all three render, all three carry an `aria-label`, text
nodes intact, and axe reports no violations across 31 pages.

## AI provenance on the forecast figure

User asked for a line under week 1 slide 13's graph saying it was AI
generated. Added: "This figure was generated with AI."

Implementation detail worth keeping: both captions moved out of the SVG into
an HTML `<figcaption>`. A `role="img"` element makes its children
presentational, so a `<text>` provenance note inside the SVG is invisible to
a screen reader — and provenance is the last thing that should depend on
seeing the picture. As HTML it's selectable, announced, and survives
`astromotion-text`.

Good instinct on the user's part to scope it to the graph rather than all
three figures. The graph is the only one making an empirical claim, so its
provenance is load-bearing; `FigCrossing` diagrams the course's own
structure and `FigSpine` diagrams a published framework, and neither could
be mistaken for sourced data. Offered to extend it to those two if wanted.

It also lines up with the course's own posture: `policies.md` requires
students to disclose and forbids AI-drafted reflections, so a course site
that quietly ships an AI-made figure would be holding itself to a lower bar
than its students.

## Finally being able to see the slides

Slide fit had been an open unknown for most of this work — `astromotion-check`
needs `puppeteer-core` and a Linux Chrome, and this machine only has the
Windows one. Turns out that's enough: WSL2 forwards localhost both ways, so
`chrome.exe --headless --screenshot` against the WSL dev server works, and the
PNG lands on a `/mnt/c` path readable from here.

    "/mnt/c/Program Files/Google/Chrome/Application/chrome.exe" \
      --headless --disable-gpu --enable-automation \
      --virtual-time-budget=14000 --window-size=1280,720 \
      --screenshot="C:\\...\\out.png" \
      "http://localhost:4321/comp4020-ass2-BilalM004/decks/week-01/#/8"

`--window-size=1280,720` matches the deck canvas exactly, so what's captured
is what's projected. `--enable-automation` sets `navigator.webdriver`, which
suppresses astromotion's first-run keyboard-hint card — without it the card
sits over the middle of the first screenshot. The long virtual-time budget
lets fonts and the reveal layout settle.

What it immediately turned up, none of which any check catches:

- Slide 13 was overflowing its padding box, so "every time" sat on top of the
  presenter tag. The user spotted this before I did. Fixed by folding the
  trailing paragraph into the figure caption, where the attribution belonged
  anyway.
- The teaching-team portraits are cream-ground artwork drawn for a light
  site. Five at full column width were the brightest thing on a near-black
  slide and out-shouted the heading. Now 148px circles.
- Circular and un-zoomed, most of each circle was empty cream — the figure
  occupies roughly a third of the source frame. Went to a clipping wrapper
  with the image scaled inside.

That last one needed an actual experiment rather than reasoning. `scale(1.75)`
looked almost identical to no zoom, and I couldn't tell from the screenshot
whether the transform was being applied at all or was just too subtle. Rather
than keep guessing I set `scale(3)` as a deliberately extreme test: it filled
the circles properly, which proved the mechanism and gave the right value in
one step. Kept 3, and bumped the requested source to 480px so a 148px circle
at 3x crops from real pixels.

Also confirmed good by eye, having never seen them before: both `action`

## Dev server: the polling gotcha has a second symptom

Knew about `CHOKIDAR_USEPOLLING=true` from 2026-09-13 — this repo is on
`/mnt/c`, so WSL never fires the inotify events Vite's watcher wants. What
was logged then was the visible symptom: edits silently don't hot-reload.

Started it without the flag today and hit a different one: the server never
finishes starting at all, dying on a 30s watchdog with
`Dev server failed to start within 30s`. The JSON shape of that error is not
Astro's, which sent me looking for a hook before the answer turned out to be
the already-known flag. With polling it comes up in 33 seconds — i.e. just
past the watchdog, which is why the failure looks like a hang rather than an
error.

So the full invocation, worth using every time rather than only when
hot-reload matters:

    CHOKIDAR_USEPOLLING=true ASTRO_DEV_BACKGROUND=1 pnpm dev

(The permission allowlist in `.claude/settings.local.json` already had this
exact form — worth reading as documentation next time.)
