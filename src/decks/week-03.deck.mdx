## No cash. On purpose.

Money doesn't price a social exchange. It ends one.

Ariely's version: people will happily help you move a couch for nothing,
and refuse to help you move it for three dollars. Introduce a price and the
favour stops being a favour — it becomes a badly paid job.

<DeckPresenter of="george-curious" />

```notes
The couch lands better than the abstract version. If they push back, the
daycare late-fine study is the other one — fining late parents made
lateness *more* common, because a fine is a price and a price is
permission.
```

---

## What a gift obliges

Mauss, writing in 1925 about gift economies, found the same three-part
obligation everywhere he looked: to give, to receive, and to repay.

A purchase creates none of them. That's the whole reason money is banned
this fortnight — a receipt settles the account, and a settled account is
the end of the relationship, not the start of one.

<DeckPresenter of="george-curious" />

## Pick your currency

**Object.** A badge, a book, half a sandwich.

**Favour.** Carrying a busker's amp between sets.

**Tip.** Something you know about this campus that they don't.

**Skill.** Proofreading, a language, fixing a bike chain.

It matters less which one than whether you'd have handed it over if nobody
were marking you.

<DeckPresenter of="george-curious" />

---

## Name your price first

You say what you're offering before you know what's coming back, and let
the other person set the rest of the rate.

Wait to see what they'll offer and you haven't made a trade. You've run an
auction.

<DeckPresenter of="george-curious" />

---

## Every trade has a rate

Whether or not you did the arithmetic beforehand — and you only find out
what it was afterwards.

[The Exchange Rate](/sessions/04-the-exchange-rate/), next week: what you
put in, what came out, and whether you'd call that fair. "Fair" isn't the
only acceptable answer.

## A boring report-back isn't a bad grade waiting to happen

It's a sign the trade needed another pass.

You can't narrate your way out of an interaction where nothing was at
stake. So stop fixing the telling, and fix the thing you're telling about.

<DeckPresenter of="yu-tobe" />

```notes
The thesis of my half. Everything after this is either "make a trade worth
telling" or "tell it".
```

## The clip

One per Crossing, five by the end, and they play as one video in week 12 —
see [Legacy](/assessments/legacy/).

A clip isn't documentation. It's the thirty seconds that prove the thing
happened to somebody who wasn't there.

It's also yours, of your Crossing — not a recording of the person you
traded with. Want them in it? Ask first, record the yes, and shoot a
version without them anyway; consent can be taken back.
[Policies](/policies/) is the short version.
