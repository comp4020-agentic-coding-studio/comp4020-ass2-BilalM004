## The idea

Why the account is the whole Crossing

## There is nothing to go and do

No stranger to approach. Nothing to trade. No stall, no ask, no partner
found in a corridor.

Crossing 6 is made entirely out of the other five. The doing this
fortnight is assembling an honest account of them — and it is not the
easier job.

## Twelve weeks already has a shape

McAdams spent a career on the life-story model of identity: people don't
carry their past as a list of things that happened, they carry a story
with a shape — and the shape gets chosen, usually by nobody in particular.

Twelve weeks isn't a life. It's long enough to have picked up a shape you
didn't decide on.

<DeckPresenter of="george-curious" />

```notes
Don't let this drift into therapy. The point is practical: the account
they'd give off the cuff today has already been edited, and they weren't
the editor.
```

---

## The shape you'll reach for

The bad bit turns out to have been worth it. McAdams calls that a
redemption sequence, and it's the most available story there is — it's
also the one he warns about, because not everything redeems and a narrator
who insists it does stops sounding honest.

Five redemptions in a row isn't an account of a semester. It's a template
with your name on it.

<DeckPresenter of="george-curious" />

---

## The semester you remember isn't the one you had

Kahneman's experiencing self and remembering self keep different books.
What you retain of something is closer to its most intense moment and how
it ended than to the hours you actually spent inside it.

So the Crossing you now think of as the good one is data. It is not the
record.

<DeckPresenter of="george-curious" />

```notes
The colonoscopy work is the hard version if anyone pushes: retrospective
ratings tracked the peak and the last few minutes, and procedures that ran
much longer weren't remembered as much worse. Keep it qualitative — I'm
not quoting coefficients at them.
```

---

## Go back to what you said at the time

Your five clips. Whatever you told the room the week after each Crossing,
while it was close enough to the moment to still be unflattering.

That's the record. What you'd say today is the edit — and the gap between
the two is the most interesting thing you've got.

<DeckPresenter of="george-curious" />

```notes
Push them to do this before the Lab, not after. The ones who rewatch the
clips first arrive with something to talk about; the ones who don't arrive
with a summary.
```

## You won't find the hole in your own head

Ask someone to rate how well they understand something, then ask them to
explain it step by step. The rating and the explanation don't match. The
explaining is what finds the gap.

Your account has only ever been explained to somebody who was there for all
of it. You.

<DeckPresenter of="george-curious" />

```notes
Rozenblit and Keil, 2002 — the illusion of explanatory depth. Their
refrigerator example lands fastest: everyone knows how one works until they
have to say how.
```

## Anecdote, then the point of it

Ira Glass's two building blocks. The anecdote: this happened, then this,
then this. The moment of reflection: somebody says what the point of it
was. Both have to be interesting, and he's blunt that a great sequence of
events with nothing said about it still fails.

Five anecdotes and no reflection is a diary. A verdict with no five
Crossings under it is an opinion.

<DeckPresenter of="george-curious" />

```notes
Cheap exercise if there's time: make someone give me one Crossing as
thirty seconds of anecdote, then one sentence of reflection. The second
part is always the one they haven't written.
```

---

## The video does the corroborating

Five clips, one per fieldwork Crossing, played in full inside your slot.
They aren't decoration at the end — they're the evidence that what you've
just claimed about each Crossing actually happened that way.

Have the file ready and tested. The slot is for presenting.

<DeckPresenter of="george-curious" />

---

## Anything with a person in it can vanish

A featured person can withdraw consent right up to the Finale, and when
they do, that footage comes out of your video.

Which is why the version without them isn't a formality — it's the clip you
actually own. [Policies](/policies/) has the three steps, in order.

<DeckPresenter of="george-curious" />

```notes
Every year somebody discovers this in week 11 with one clip and no spare.
Ask, out loud, who is relying on footage of another person for a Crossing,
and make them fix it this week.
```
