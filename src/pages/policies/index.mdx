# Policies and support

## Missing a Crossing

The report and reflection at a Crossing are always live, in the room — that
part has no substitute. But the hallway work itself doesn't have to happen
at the exact moment everyone else's does: if you can't be there for it that
week, do your own version of it solo, in your own time, before the
Crossing, and record evidence (a photo, a voice note, a screenshot of the
exchange — whatever actually fits what you did). Bring that to the
Crossing and report on it the same as anyone else would. The recording
isn't a substitute for showing up — it's what stands in for the group
having seen it happen.

That solo fallback is for Crossings you do alone. Building With Others runs
on a team, so it doesn't work the same way there — if you can't make a
live session, that's a conversation with your team, not a solo workaround.
Sort out between yourselves what still gets done and how, and stick to it:
this course is built around campus, in person, so whatever the team agrees
still has to happen on site, between the people actually on the team, not
move online because that's easier for one person's schedule.

Neither fallback covers everything — if something genuinely unavoidable
comes up that these don't fit, contact us before the Crossing and we'll
work out a substitution that fits the situation.

## Late work and extensions

There's no such thing as a late submission on this site — every mark comes
from being in the room, on the day, presenting or participating live.
Miss the Crossing itself (or, for the Midsemester Checkpoint and Legacy,
the Showcase or Finale slot) and that mark is missed — there's no
submitting after the fact to recover it. The one exception is the
genuinely-unavoidable case described above under Missing a Crossing:
contact us before the Crossing, not after, and we'll work out a
substitution — that's the only route back to a missed mark, and it isn't
available once the Crossing has already happened.

## Academic integrity

Your reflections have to be yours: written or spoken by you, about
something that actually happened to you, with no AI tool drafting,
rewriting, or generating any part of them. The whole premise of this
course is that the work is your own account of your own week — an
AI-assisted reflection isn't a shortcut around the mark, it's a different,
ungradable thing, because there's no real experience behind it to check.
The same goes for reporting an interaction that didn't happen, or wasn't
yours. If you're ever unsure whether something counts, ask before the
Crossing, not during it.

## What actually gets checked

Every Crossing and every assessment on this site names, in its own spec,
exactly what's being looked at when it's marked — nothing here is judged
against a rubric that isn't written down somewhere on its own page. If you
want to know precisely what a marker is checking before you stand up, read
that page's spec list, not this one.

## Getting help

Questions about a specific Crossing or assessment go to whoever's teaching
that week — see [People](/people/) for who that is. Questions about these
policies themselves go to the convenor.
