# Policies and support

## Missing a Crossing

The report and reflection at a Crossing are always live, in the room — that
part has no substitute. But the hallway work itself doesn't have to happen
at the exact moment everyone else's does: if you can't be there for it that
week, do your own version of it solo, in your own time, before the
Crossing, and record evidence (a photo, a voice note, a screenshot of the
exchange — whatever actually fits what you did). Bring that to the
Crossing and report on it the same as anyone else would. The recording
isn't a substitute for showing up — it's what stands in for the group
having seen it happen.

That evidence is a different thing from the clip. The clip is owed for
every fieldwork Crossing whether or not you were in step with everyone
else; this evidence is only needed when you weren't. If you film the work
you did solo, one recording can do both jobs — but a photo or a voice note
covers the evidence and still leaves you a clip to shoot.

That solo fallback is for Crossings you do alone, and two Crossings don't
fit it the same way.

Set Up Shop runs on a team, assigned for the Crossing, so if you can't
make a stall session, that's a conversation with your team, not a solo
workaround: sort out between yourselves what still gets covered and how,
and stick to it. The stall still has to run on site, staffed by whoever's
actually there, not move online because that's easier for one person's
schedule.

Building With Others has no team to fall back on — the partner is found
fresh in the hallway each week, so if you can't make the live session,
that's a conversation with us beforehand, not a solo workaround, since
there's no one lined up in advance to cover for you. Whatever we agree
still has to happen on site, between two people actually there together.

Neither fallback covers everything — if something genuinely unavoidable
comes up that these don't fit, contact us before the Crossing and we'll
work out a substitution that fits the situation.

## Late work and extensions

There's no such thing as a late submission on this site — every mark comes
from being in the room, on the day, presenting or participating live.
Miss the Crossing itself (or, for the Midsemester Checkpoint and Legacy,
the Showcase or Finale slot) and that mark is missed — there's no
submitting after the fact to recover it. The one exception is the
genuinely-unavoidable case described above under Missing a Crossing:
contact us before the Crossing, not after, and we'll work out a
substitution — that's the only route back to a missed mark, and it isn't
available once the Crossing has already happened.

## Filming and consent

Every fieldwork Crossing asks you to come away with a short clip, and the
five of them play as one video at the Finale — see
[Legacy](/assessments/legacy/) for what the set has to do.

That clip is your own record of your own Crossing. It is not a recording of
the person you crossed with, and nothing on this course asks you to point a
camera at anybody. Film the object, the trade, the stall, the thing you
built, the place it happened, or yourself describing it straight afterwards.
People who are merely in shot — passers-by, the queue behind you, whoever
happens to be in a room — need nothing from you.

Featuring someone is different. Featured means they are part of what the
clip is about: identifiable, filmed deliberately, and there for more than a
passing moment. Three steps, in this order:

1. **Ask first.** Before you record anything of them, not partway through.
2. **If they agree, record the consent too** — their answer, kept on the
   record alongside the footage it covers.
3. **Record something else as well** — a clip of that Crossing that stands
   on its own without them in it.

Step three is the one people skip, and it is the one that protects you.
Consent can be withdrawn at any point up to the Finale, and when it is, that
footage comes out of your video. So anything featuring another person is
supplementary: it can make a clip better, and it can never be the only clip
you hold for that Crossing. Shoot nothing but the featured version, have it
withdrawn in week 11, and you have nothing left to play.

A no at step one costs you nothing either, for the same reason — step three
is your clip regardless.

## Academic integrity

Your reflections have to be yours: written or spoken by you, about
something that actually happened to you, with no AI tool drafting,
rewriting, or generating any part of them. The whole premise of this
course is that the work is your own account of your own week — an
AI-assisted reflection isn't a shortcut around the mark, it's a different,
ungradable thing, because there's no real experience behind it to check.
The same goes for reporting an interaction that didn't happen, or wasn't
yours. If you're ever unsure whether something counts, ask before the
Crossing, not during it.

## What actually gets checked

Every Crossing and every assessment on this site names, in its own spec,
exactly what's being looked at when it's marked — nothing here is judged
against a rubric that isn't written down somewhere on its own page. If you
want to know precisely what a marker is checking before you stand up, read
that page's spec list, not this one.

## Getting help

Questions about a specific Crossing or assessment go to whoever's teaching
that week — see [People](/people/) for who that is. Questions about these
policies themselves go to the convenor.
