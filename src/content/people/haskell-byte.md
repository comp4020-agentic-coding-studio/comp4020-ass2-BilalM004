Haskell spent most of undergrad talking to compilers, which are patient and
tell you exactly what's wrong --- people do neither, and a PhD in
distributed systems didn't teach them what to say at the edge of a room
where everyone already seems to know each other. So they did what a field
that's barely social by nature tends to do with a thing that won't work:
took it apart until it did. "Start a conversation with a stranger" stops
being a personality trait once you treat it as a protocol --- a reason to
be standing there, an opener that isn't "so, what do you do", an exit that
doesn't slam the door. None of it came naturally; all of it turned out to
be learnable, which is the whole case for Crossing 1. They still
over-prepare for small talk, and will hand you a better opener than the one
you walked in with.
