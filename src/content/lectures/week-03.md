Yu Tobe takes this one alone, and opens by pointing at what happened the
week before: some report-backs held the room and some died on the way out.
Yu's argument is that the difference was almost never the telling. It was
the trade.

## Why it's shaped this way

Crossing 1 was free. You spent attention and some nerve, and if it went
badly you walked away no worse off. Crossing 2 has a price: you put a real
thing up — an object, a favour, a tip, a skill — before you know what's
coming back, and you let the other person set the rest of the rate.

Money is banned, and that's the interesting constraint rather than an
arbitrary one. A price doesn't value a social exchange, it ends one: Ariely
is the evidence, with people who'll cheerfully help you move a couch for
free and refuse to do it for three dollars. Mauss is the framework — a gift
obliges you to give, to receive and to repay, and none of those survive
being settled in cash. [Make a Trade](/sessions/03-make-a-trade/) runs on
the other set of rules entirely.

## The half about telling it

This is also where the video stops being a distant requirement. Yu spent
years behind a camera and teaches the report-back the way a storyboard gets
built: what you wanted, what you offered instead, and the bit you'd cut if
you only had thirty seconds. Those are the three questions the room asks at
[The Exchange Rate](/sessions/04-the-exchange-rate/), and they're worth
running against the trade you're planning rather than the one you've
already made.

It closes by sending you back to the clip you shot in Crossing 1 with one
question: would you watch it if it weren't yours? Five of those play in
full at the end of semester — see [Legacy](/assessments/legacy/) — so the
standard is worth setting now rather than in week 11.

## What you should leave knowing

- why no money is the constraint that makes the Crossing work, not a
  restriction on it
- how to name what you're offering before you know what you'll get
- three questions that tell you whether a trade is worth reporting
- what a clip has to do, and what yours currently does instead
