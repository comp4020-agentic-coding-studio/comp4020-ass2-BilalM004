A "no" is data, not a verdict — that's the line Crossing 4 is built to
test, and this is where you find out if you actually believe it once
you're standing in front of the room saying what happened.

## In the room

Report the ask itself first — what you actually said, to whom, and why
that moment — then the answer, however it landed. A yes doesn't excuse you
from the report; the room still wants to know what you risked to get it.
A no doesn't need softening either: say what was said, not a version
where it stung less than it did.

## What gets asked

The follow-up usually lands on the ask, not the answer: what you'd change
about how you asked, whether you'd have pushed back on a no, what you'd
have lost by staying quiet instead. See [Crossing Participation](/assessments/crit-participation/)
for how the live report and reflection are marked.
