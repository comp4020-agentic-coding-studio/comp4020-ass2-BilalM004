Last week you said something to a stranger. This week you say what
happened — out loud, to the group, without having polished it since. That's
the trade Crossing 1 is really teaching: attention spent has to be
accounted for, and the accounting is worth more raw than rehearsed.

## In the room

Short, live reports, one student at a time: who you approached, what you
actually said, what came back, and whether it was worth the walk over. A
report that's clearly been tidied up since Monday reads as exactly that —
the room is listening for the version you'd have given five minutes after
it happened, hesitations included.

## What gets asked

Someone in the room will ask a follow-up — what you'd have done if they'd
said no, whether you'd approach the same way again, what you noticed about
yourself mid-conversation. That's not a stress test bolted onto the report;
it's the other half of it. See [Crossing Participation](/assessments/crit-participation/)
for how the Crossing's mark actually gets made.
