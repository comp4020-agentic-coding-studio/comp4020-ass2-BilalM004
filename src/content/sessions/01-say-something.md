Attention is the first currency this course spends, and the cheapest way to
find out what a stranger's is worth is to ask for a minute of it. That's the
whole of Crossing 1: no stall, no pitch, no assignment to point to — just
you, someone you don't know, and an opener that has to survive contact.

## The brief

> Walk up to someone you don't know and start a conversation that isn't
> about this course.

The person at the same library table every week. Whoever's ahead of you at
the coffee cart with nothing to do but wait. Someone standing at the edge of
a room where everyone else already seems to know each other — that edge is
where Crossing 1 actually happens. "So, what do you do?" is a start; a real
reason to be talking to them is what makes it land. If the honest answer to
"why did you say hello" is "for the mark," find a different opener.

## What you carry into next week

Nothing written. Just the conversation itself, close enough to the moment
that you can still say exactly what you asked, what surprised you, and
what it cost you to walk over — [How It Landed](/sessions/02-how-it-landed/)
is where you tell the room. Come away with a short clip too — the first of
the five [Legacy](/assessments/legacy/) asks for at the end. It's your
record of your Crossing, not a recording of them; if you want them in it,
[Policies](/policies/) says what that takes.
