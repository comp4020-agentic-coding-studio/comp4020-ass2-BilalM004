# Voice by page type

The site-wide default (see `CLAUDE.md`): sincere and deadpan, specific campus
detail over an abstract claim, snappy rather than rambling — but every page
still has to leave a reader clear on the what and the why.

Within that default, register shifts by what the page is for:

## Home

Pitch — sells the semester. The one place personality is front and center.

## Sessions (Crossings)

States the objective and motive — why this Crossing matters — and exactly
what's required, so a student is never unsure of the point or the task in
front of them.

## Assessments

Contract register: precise, deadpan, "here's the brief, here's what's
checked." Only turns reflective where it's describing what a student's own
reflection sounds like (close to the moment, not tidied up after) — the page
itself stays a contract, not a reflection.

## Lectures

Explains motive, objective and outcomes, not just what was covered. The
linked slides pair sourced, practical information with personality of their
own — not a bare bullet outline.

## People

A real bio with personality for each person, plus the practical part a
student actually needs: contact info and the escalation path — who to ask
first, who to ask when that doesn't work.

## Policies

The driest page on the site, deliberately: plain rules language, no jokes, no
color. Students need to trust this one literally.

---

Home and Policies are the two poles — most personality, least. Judgment
calls this file can't settle mechanically (contradictions, drift from a
page's register, a policy mismatch) are what the `content-review` skill
checks before a commit that touches session, assessment, lecture, or policy
copy.
